PART 1: MULTIPLICATION OF A VECTOR BY A SCALAR
When given k(vec)a, k is the scalar (coefficiant) and (vec)a is the non-zero vector

If k > 0, then k(vec)a will have the same DIRECTION as (vec)a, and has a magnitude of the scalar times the magnitude of (vec)a; k|vec)a|
(lesson 2 - pic 1.png)

If k < 0, then k(vec)a will have the OPPOSITE DIRECTION as (vec)a, and has a  magnitude of magnitude of the scalar times the magnitude of (vec)a; |k||vec)a|
(lesson 2 - pic 2.png)

NOTE: If you multiply the vector by a saclar of 0, you get the ZERO VECTOR
(lesson 2 - pic 3.png)

If 2 or more vectors are nonzero scalar mulriples of each other ((vec)v = k(vec)w), they are considered COLLINEAR.

NOTES: Collinear is used interchangeably with parallel, it essentaily means on the same line, in other words COINCIDENT LINES.

Linear Dependancy:
    if (vec)a and (vec)b are collinear, then (vec)a = k(vec)b
    if k > 0, that means that the vectors have the same DIRECTION
    if k < 0, that means that the vectors have the OPPOSITE DIRECTION

Important definition:
A UNIT VECTOR ((vec)u) is a vector with a magnitude of 1.
If |(vec)a| = 4 then the collinear unit vector would be, (vec)u = 1/4 (vec)a


LESSON 1: INTRODUCTION TO VECTORS

PART 1
A vector is something with both direction and magnitude. 
Its length represents its magnitude (size), where the direction the vector points in represents the direection it moves in.
Opposite vectors are parallel with the same magnitude but opposite direction.
(lesson 1 - pic 1.png)

The magnitude of a vector is represented by two pipes around it. Ideally, on paper your vector would have a little arrow on top.
But due to the nature of this computerized game and the subsequent experience of the creators, they will be represented with an arrow before.
This is a vector: (vec)AB
This means we're speaking of the magnitude of (vec)AB: |(vec)AB|

They can be represented geometrically or algebraically (on a cartesian plane).
Note DIRECTION:
Bearings are measured from North in a clockwise direction.
Direction is stated as the number of degrees east or west of north or south. I.e [N78(deg)W].

Vectors are only equal if they have the same MAGNITUDE and DIRECTION. Magnitudes are always positive.

ADDING/SUBTRACTING ALGEBRAIC VECTORS:
An algebraic vectors means a vector that is represented on a cartesian plane, in other words coordinates.

When given two algebraic vectors, (vec)a = (x(sb1),y(sb1)) and (vec)b = (x(sb2),y(sb2)), you combine the x the y of both coordinates:
Adding the vectors (vec)a + (vec)b: (x(sb1) + x(sb1), y(sb1) + (y(sb2))
Subtracting the vectors (vec)a - (vec)b: (x(sb1) + x(sb1), y(sb1) + (y(sb2))

The resultant would be the final coordinate after combining the coordinates.

A POSTION VECTOR of point P is the vector (vec)OP with the tail at the origin O, and the tip at point P. Essentialy the position vector is the vector from the origin (tail) to the tip

NOTE: The direction of an algebraic vector is ALWAYS the angle from the postive x-axis rotated counter-clockwise to the vector

HOW TO FIND A POSITION VECTOR:
A cartesian vector is a vector with end points, and neither of them is (0,0), to turn the cartesion vector into the postion vector, we essentialy have to translate the tail to (0,0).
This is also used to easily calculate the magnitude.
Say we are give (vec)AB where (vec)A = [-4,-2] and (vec)B = [-1,2], how would we turn this into a position vector?
(lesson 3 - pic 8.png)
We have the red line, and are trying to turn it into the blue line, to do this is very simple.
To get the coordinates of the position vector all you simply have to do is, the head - the tail and squaring it, esentialy the coordinates of B - A squared (the order is VERY important)
(vec)AB = [(-1-(-4))(ss2),(2-(-2))(ss2)]
(vec)AB = [3,4]

1. THEORETICAL PROBABILITY

Theoretical probability is the ratio (fraction) of the number of outcomes that make up an event to the total number of possible outcomes.

The formula is P(A) = n(A)/n(S),

P = the probability
n = number
S = sample (total options)
A = event (what we are looking for)

When using the formula you would sub in the actual names.

Example: At Merivale HS there are: 411 Gr12, 391 Gr11, 380 Gr10, 380 Gr9, what is the probability of senior students?

senior - means Gr 12 and 11

P(senior) = n(senior)/n(students)
P(senior) = (411 + 391)/(411 + 391 + 380 + 380)
P(senior) = 802/1562 , since its a probability its fine to not reduce the fraction

We can also find the probability of a complementray event:
P(event not happening) = total - P(event happens)

Normaly for the P(event not happening), we use this ' symbol to represent it.

NOTE: 
      - Something to keep in mind is that if you are dealing with percents sub in 100 as the total, if you are dealing with fractions sub in 1 as your total,
      - If the question gives percents/fractions use the formula as is, BUT if the questions is written with whole numbers, change the P's into n's, since its not a probability

Example: There is a 60% chance it rains, what is the probability of it NOT raining?

P(rain)' = 100% - P(rain)
P(rain)' = 100% - 60%
P(rain)' = 40%

Example: There are 14 students in a class of 30 with long hair, what is the probability of students NOT having long hair?

n(long hair)' = n(total) - n(long hair)
n(long hair)' = 30 - 14
n(long hair)' = 16

2. THE ADDITIVE PRINCIPLE AND MUTTUALLY EXCLUSIVE EVENTS

Formulas:
        n(A (union) B) = n(A) + n(B) - n(A (int) B)
        P(A (union) B) = P(A) + P(B) - P(A (int) B)

The (union) symbol reprents a union (OR)
The (int) symbol represents an intersection (AND)

Muttually exclusive - means that the events can't happen at the same time

If an event is muttually exclusive then, n(A (int) B)/P(A (int) B) would equal 0.

Example: There are 16 boys on the soccer team and 28 boys on the rugby team. 9 students play on both teams. How many boys are on either of the two teams?
n(S (union) R) = n(S) + n(R) - n(S (int) R)
n(S (union) R) = 16 + 28 -9
n(S (union) R) = 35 boys

Example: The probability of event A is 3/10, the probability of event B is 2/5. The probability of event A or B occurring is 7/10. Are these events mutually exclusive?
P(A (union) B) = P(A) + P(B) - P(A (int) B)
7/10 = 3/10 + 2/5 - P(A (int) B)
7/10 = 7/10 - P(A (int) B)
P(A (int) B) = 0 - yes mutually exclusive
